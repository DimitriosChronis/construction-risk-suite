The 15% monthly volatility cap applied in the Monte Carlo simulation is empirically grounded in the ELSTAT data rather than derived from contractual norms (e.g., FIDIC). The cap constrains the fitted monthly standard deviation parameter sigma used in each series' log-normal marginal distribution. Across the five construction cost indices over the 2000-2024 study period (n=299 monthly observations), the maximum observed absolute monthly log-return was 10.31% (Fuel_Energy), and the highest fitted sigma was 1.95% (Fuel_Energy) -- representing only 13.0% of the 15% cap (Table X). No index ever recorded a monthly return exceeding the cap threshold, and no fitted sigma parameter approached it. The cap therefore functions as a numerical safeguard against simulation pathologies rather than as a binding empirical constraint. This data-driven verification replaces a previously FIDIC-referenced justification with a statistically demonstrable bound: the 15% threshold lies at the 100th empirical percentile of all observed monthly price movements, providing a conservative but non-distorting upper bound on simulation volatility.
